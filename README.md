# My Wallet – GRC Practical Project

## Project Overview

This project presents a simulated Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) assessment for a fictional digital wallet company, **My Wallet**, planning to introduce a customer-to-customer money transfer feature.

The project focuses on identifying business and operational risks, reviewing applicable legal and regulatory obligations, assessing internal controls, and supporting an informed launch decision.

**Project Type:** Simulated GRC Case Study  
**Role:** GRC Analyst  
**Industry:** Digital Payments / FinTech  
**Country Context:** Jordan

## Business Scenario

My Wallet plans to introduce a new money transfer feature to improve customer convenience and support business growth.

However, the company faces challenges related to risk management, regulatory compliance, and control assessment. The project proposes a structured GRC approach to help the company identify potential issues, define responsibilities, assess control effectiveness, and evaluate launch readiness.

## Project Objectives

- Identify and assess risks associated with the money transfer feature.
- Define risk appetite and governance responsibilities.
- Identify applicable legal and regulatory requirements.
- Assess existing controls and identify potential gaps.
- Evaluate residual risks after considering verified controls.
- Support management's launch decision.
- Establish an approach for ongoing monitoring and continuous improvement.

## Project Scope and Methodology

The project follows eight stages:

1. **Objectives and Risk Appetite** – Define business objectives and acceptable risk levels.
2. **Governance** – Establish roles, responsibilities, and decision-making authority.
3. **Risk Management** – Identify key risks and propose risk treatment actions.
4. **Compliance** – Identify legal and regulatory requirements and develop a compliance obligations register.
5. **Control Assessment** – Review existing controls and identify control gaps.
6. **Residual Risk Assessment** – Reassess risks after considering verified controls.
7. **Launch Readiness and Decision** – Consolidate findings and support management's launch decision.
8. **Monitoring and Continuous Improvement** – Establish ongoing monitoring and review processes.

## Key Risks Identified

The project focuses on three primary risks:

- **Fraud:** Unauthorized or fraudulent transactions that may cause financial losses and damage customer trust.
- **Transfer Errors:** Incorrect transfers that may result in customer losses, complaints, and operational costs.
- **Service Outages:** Service disruptions that may prevent customers from transferring money or accessing wallet services.

## Compliance Approach

The project identifies regulatory areas requiring review, including:

- Licensing and authorization.
- Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing (AML/CFT).
- Financial consumer protection.
- Technical requirements and cybersecurity.
- Governance and compliance responsibilities.
- Personal data protection.

ISO 37301 is used as a reference for structuring the compliance management approach. Its use does not imply that the fictional company has implemented the standard or obtained certification.

The applicability and current status of relevant Jordanian laws and regulatory instructions must be verified before any compliance conclusions are made.

## Important Note

This is a **simulated educational portfolio project**. My Wallet is a fictional company, and the project does not represent an actual regulatory assessment, audit, or implementation engagement.

No claim is made that controls have been implemented, compliance has been achieved, or the money transfer feature is ready for launch. Such conclusions would require verified evidence and an assessment of the actual operating environment.
